California Security Deposit Limit (AB 12): One Month's Rent, the Small-Landlord Exception and the 21-Day Return

Since July 1, 2024, a California landlord generally may not demand or receive a security deposit of more than one month's rent, in addition to the first month's rent (Civil Code §1950.5(c), as amended by AB 12). A landlord who is a natural person, or an LLC whose members are all natural persons, and who owns no more than two residential rental properties with no more than four units in total may collect up to two months, unless the tenant is a service member. After the tenant moves out, the landlord has 21 days to return the deposit with an itemized statement of any deductions (§1950.5(h)).

Last reviewed: November 12, 2026. General information, not legal or tax advice. For your specific situation, talk to a California attorney.

1. What counts toward the cap

"Security" in §1950.5(b) means any payment, fee, deposit or charge imposed at the beginning of the tenancy, including one imposed as an advance payment of rent, whatever it is called. That includes a pet deposit, a "key deposit" or a "cleaning deposit." The first month's rent is not counted. The cap is the same for furnished and unfurnished units. Application screening fees are handled separately under §1950.6.

2. The small-landlord exception

Up to two months' rent is allowed only if both are true (§1950.5(c)(5)):

  • The landlord is a natural person or an LLC in which all members are natural persons. A settlor or beneficiary of a family trust counts as a natural person.
  • The landlord owns no more than two residential rental properties that together include no more than four units offered for rent.

The exception does not apply if the prospective tenant is a service member, and the landlord may not refuse to rent to a service member because of that. Deposits collected before July 1, 2024 are not affected by the new cap.

3. Prepaid rent

Rent paid in advance counts as security. The one exception: §1950.5(c)(2) "does not prohibit an advance payment of not less than six months' rent if the term of the lease is six months or longer." So for a 12-month lease, prepaying 6 to 12 months is allowed; prepaying 2 or 3 months on top of a full deposit generally exceeds the cap. This comes up often with international students and new arrivals who have no U.S. credit history. Asking for more only from applicants of a certain national origin or immigration status would be discrimination.

4. Returning the deposit

  • Deadline: no later than 21 calendar days after the tenant vacates, return the balance and an itemized statement (§1950.5(h)(1)).
  • Allowed deductions: unpaid rent, damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, and cleaning needed to return the unit to its move-in level of cleanliness. No deductions for conditions that existed before the tenancy or for ordinary wear and tear (§1950.5(b), (e)).
  • Documentation: invoices or receipts for work done by others; time and hourly rate for work the landlord did. Receipts are not required if repairs and cleaning together are $125 or less, unless the tenant asks within 14 days.
  • Photos: for tenancies beginning on or after July 1, 2025, photos at move-in; since April 1, 2025, photos after move-out and after repairs or cleaning, provided with any repair or cleaning deduction (§1950.5(g), (h)(2)(D)).
  • Payment method: if the tenant paid the deposit or rent electronically, the balance generally must be returned electronically unless agreed otherwise in writing.
  • Inspection: the tenant may request an initial inspection before moving out (§1950.5(f)).
  • Bad faith: keeping a deposit in bad faith can lead to statutory damages of up to twice the deposit plus actual damages (§1950.5(m)).

What to do

  1. Count how many rental properties and units you own before setting the deposit.
  2. Add up every move-in charge, not just the line called "deposit."
  3. Photograph the unit at move-in and send the tenant a copy.
  4. Calendar the 21-day deadline as soon as you get the keys back.
  5. Keep receipts for every deduction.

Example (hypothetical, not a real client)

Two owners each rent out a condo for $3,200 a month. Owner A, an individual whose only rental is this condo, may collect up to $6,400 in security (two months), unless the tenant is a service member. Owner B owns three rental properties, so the cap is $3,200. When Owner B's tenant moves out, there is $280 of carpet cleaning beyond ordinary wear and tear, documented by photos and an invoice. Owner B mails the itemized statement, invoice and a check for $2,920 within 21 days.

Frequently asked questions

Since July 1, 2024, generally no more than one month's rent in addition to the first month's rent, for furnished or unfurnished units. Qualifying small landlords may collect up to two months unless the tenant is a service member.

A natural person (including a family trust settlor or beneficiary) or an LLC whose members are all natural persons, who owns no more than two residential rental properties with no more than four units offered for rent in total.

21 calendar days after the tenant moves out, together with an itemized statement of any deductions and copies of receipts or invoices for repairs and cleaning.

Prepaid rent counts as security. The exception is an advance payment of at least six months' rent on a lease of six months or longer. Smaller prepayments, such as two or three months, count toward the one-month cap.
