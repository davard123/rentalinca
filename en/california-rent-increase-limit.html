California Rent Increase Limit 2026: How Much Can a Landlord Raise Rent?

For most California rentals covered by AB 1482, a landlord may raise rent by no more than 5% plus the regional CPI change, or 10%, whichever is lower, in any 12-month period (Civil Code §1947.12). For Los Angeles and Orange County, the California Attorney General lists the maximum as 8.7% for increases effective August 1, 2026 to July 31, 2027 (8% for the year before). The landlord must also give written notice at least 30 days ahead for an increase of 10% or less, and at least 90 days ahead for an increase over 10% (Civil Code §827).

1. The state cap and the current numbers

The cap is calculated on the lowest rent charged in the 12 months before the increase takes effect, and a tenant who stays can be given at most two increases in 12 months (Civil Code §1947.12(a)). The CPI used is the CPI-U for the property's metro area. The Attorney General turns that into one percentage per area on oag.ca.gov/rentcaps:

AreaEffective Aug 1, 2025 – Jul 31, 2026Effective Aug 1, 2026 – Jul 31, 2027
Los Angeles County, Orange County8%8.7%
Riverside County, San Bernardino County7.5%8.1%
San Diego County8.8%8.2%

The percentage that applies is the one for the date the increase takes effect, not the date the notice is served. When a unit is re-rented to a new tenant and no prior tenant remains, the owner may set the new starting rent without the cap (§1947.12(b)).

2. When the cap does not apply

The cap does not apply to exempt properties. The most common ones are housing issued a certificate of occupancy within the previous 15 years, owner-occupied duplexes, and single-family homes or condos owned by individuals (not a REIT, corporation, or LLC with a corporate member) when the tenant received the statutory exemption notice. Details: AB 1482 exemption notice and what AB 1482 covers.

Exempt does not mean unlimited notice-free increases. The §827 notice periods below still apply to every month-to-month residential tenancy.

3. How much notice: 30 or 90 days (Civil Code §827)

  • 10% or less (counting all increases in the prior 12 months): written notice at least 30 days before the increase takes effect.
  • More than 10% (alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months): at least 90 days.
  • Delivery: personal delivery, or by mail under Code of Civil Procedure §1013, which adds 5 days when mailed within California. The Attorney General notes that a call, text or email is not formal written notice.
  • Fixed-term leases: §827 covers month-to-month and other periodic tenancies. During a fixed-term lease, the rent is whatever the lease says, so increases usually come at renewal unless the lease itself provides for one.

An increase also cannot be retaliation, for example for complaining about repairs or habitability (Civil Code §1942.5).

4. Local rent control can be stricter

Where a city ordinance limits increases to less than the state cap, the local limit controls for the units it covers. The Attorney General's chart lists, among others:

  • City of Los Angeles (RSO units): 3% for July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2027.
  • Santa Ana: 2.87% for September 1, 2026 – August 31, 2027 (city renter protections page).
  • Pasadena, Santa Monica, West Hollywood, Inglewood, Baldwin Park and others with their own formulas.

The chart does not list a local rent control ordinance for Irvine, so in Irvine the state rules are the main limit. Ordinances change; confirm with the city before sending a notice.

What to do before raising rent

  1. Check whether the unit is covered by AB 1482 or a local ordinance.
  2. Find the lowest rent charged in the last 12 months and any increase already given.
  3. Apply the percentage for the effective date and round down, not up.
  4. Pick the notice period (30 or 90 days, plus 5 if mailed) and serve it in writing.
  5. Look at the market too: the cap is a ceiling, not a target. Recent leases of comparable units matter more than asking prices.

Example (hypothetical, not a real client)

A covered Orange County apartment rents for $3,000, unchanged for the past 12 months. For an increase effective October 1, 2026, the cap is 8.7%: $3,000 × 1.087 = $3,261. The owner may raise rent to $3,261 or less with at least 30 days' written notice (35 if mailed). If the same unit were exempt and the owner wanted $3,400 (a 13.3% increase), no state cap would apply, but the notice would have to be served at least 90 days ahead.
